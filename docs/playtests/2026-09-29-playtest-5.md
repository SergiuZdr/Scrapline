### What each combat point means in the rules today
- **Shove (PT5-4)**: `_execute_attack` shoves only a victim still alive after the hit.
- **Paths (PT5-5)**: scrap piles are open ground (cost 1); rubble and ridges cost 2. Among
  equal-cost paths the pathfinder takes the first by row, then column -- so even a route
  through rubble that costs the same as the way round can come out as a detour.
- **Pierce (PT5-6, PT5-9)**: `pierce N` lets the beam pass through N machines and hit the one
  after (a lance, pierce 1, hits two in a line; Double Pierce, 2, hits three; the rail driver,
  9, everything), then carries 2 hexes past its range at half damage. **Props stop it**: a drum
  or a crate ends the beam.
- **Chain (PT5-7, PT5-8)**: from the first thing hit, the arc jumps to an *adjacent* hex only,
  preferring any enemy, then a drum, then a crate, then anyone -- first found, not best. A heap
  is not a target, and a jump is one hex, so an arc can never cross one.
- **Undo (PT5-10)**: `_undo` replays the fight and keeps `_selected` as it was -- which after an
  attack is usually the *next* machine, picked automatically, or none.

## What this says about the process
- The look pass (010) measured contrast and followed a style bible, and still produced a game
  the user calls generic. **Photographed PBR was the wrong base, however well measured**: it
  pushed the game toward realism, and realism is where procedural box geometry looks cheapest.
  The next look has to be a *style*, chosen by the user from concrete options, and judged in a
  single style frame before it touches every screen.
