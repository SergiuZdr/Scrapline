# Scrapline — art direction, from reference

The reference sheets in this folder are the target. Everything below is read off them,
so it can be checked against them rather than argued about.

| File | What it fixes |
|---|---|
| `worker_unit.png` | Palette, proportion, the asymmetric-arm rule |
| `brawler_03_and_arena.png` | A second frame in the same language, and the ARENA |

**Why this file exists.** Four visual passes were made on this project without a named
reference set, and every one of them optimised toward "clean" — which, with no target,
means grey. A style is not "not-realistic"; it is a specific set of materials,
proportions and devices that can be scored against.

## The palette

Every sheet lists the same materials. There is no blue in any of them.

| Material | Where it goes | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Worn paint — yellow** | Large armour plates | The identity colour. Construction yellow, chipped through to metal at every edge |
| **Worn paint — red** | Large armour plates | The second livery. Oxide red, same treatment |
| **Worn paint — green** | Large armour plates | Third livery, olive |
| **Rusted steel** | Ground contact, heat, old welds | Warm orange-brown |
| **Dark steel** | Recesses, mechanism, joints | Near black |
| **Dirty aluminium** | Pistons, rams, cylinders | **The light value.** Nothing else in the palette is bright |
| **Rubber** | Hoses, treads, bushings | Matte black |
| **Copper** | Fittings, glands, small pipework | Small areas only |
| **Glass, dirty** | Lenses | |

**`dirty aluminium` is the finding that matters most.** The reference machines read
because they carry a genuinely LIGHT material against near-black mechanism. The current
roster has no light value anywhere, which is why it reads muddy however the lights are
tuned. Polished rams and cylinders are where it goes.

**Paint is never clean and never flat.** Every painted panel in the reference is chipped
along its edges, scratched across its face, and stained downward from its fixings.

## Proportion

Scale is stated on the sheets: **2.1–2.2 m**, drawn against a human silhouette.

- **Torso is wide and boxy**, with the engine visible on it — not a smooth shell
- **Shoulders are broad**, carrying separate pauldron plates
- **Limbs are thick and segmented**, with a visible piston, ram or spring at every joint
- **Feet are big flat plates**, far wider than the ankle above them
- **The head is small and sits LOW**, down between the shoulders, with one or two warm
  glowing lenses
- **A backpack or fuel tank sits on the back** as a visible power source
- **The arms do not match.** One is the heavy tool — chainsaw, saw, hammer — and the
  other is a claw or grapple

Measured against this, the current roster is too thin, its feet are far too small, its
head sits too high on a neck, and its limbs are smooth where they should show mechanism.

## Identity devices

- **A stencilled two-digit number on the torso** (`12`, `07`, `03`). The cheapest
  per-machine identity on the sheet and it survives any distance
- **Warm amber eye lenses**, the only emissive on the machine
- Asymmetric arms, as above — the silhouette says what the machine does

## The arena

`brawler_03_and_arena.png` is a 4v4 pit called THE JUNKYARD, and it is **overcast
daylight, not night**:

- A circular arena with numbered team banners (01–04) on the walls
- Crane with a grab claw, a moving press, explosive barrels — hazards that move
- Scrap piles as cover, elevated walkways, marked spawn rings on the floor

This is the one place the reference and the shipped game disagree. Scrapline's current
palette doctrine is "warm sodium key at night", and everything — the yard, the hub, the
fog, the light rig — is built to it. The reference is a daylight pit. **That is a
decision to take deliberately, not to drift into.**
